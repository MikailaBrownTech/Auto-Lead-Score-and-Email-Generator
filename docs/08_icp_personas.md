# ICP personas. Mark unverified items HYPOTHESIS until real conversations confirm them.

PERSONA: CPA firm managing partner (5-20 staff)

Cares about: busy season, client trust, malpractice insurance renewals, not being the cause of a breach
Triggers: insurer asks for a WISP, new hire, phishing scare, a peer got hit 
Best angle: "your insurer or the IRS will ask for a written plan" (liability, not tech)
Best CTA: 2-minute scorecard
Likely objections: "our IT guy handles it", "we're too small", "too busy in season"
Language: clients, engagement, the firm, compliance, peer review
Avoid: EDR, SOC, zero trust, fear language

PERSONA: Solo or small tax preparer

Cares about: getting through filing season without a disaster; keeping their PTIN, EFIN, and reputation intact; client trust (referral and repeat business); not getting hit with an IRS or FTC penalty they can't absorb; doing this alone with no compliance or IT staff
Triggers: EFIN or PTIN renewal, IRS Security Summit reminders, a client's return rejected because someone already filed under their SSN, a phishing email posing as IRS/e-Services/software vendor, a local peer reporting a breach, switching tax software or adding a seasonal employee 
Best angle: IRS Pub 4557 and the written information security plan requirement (frame it as something doable in an afternoon, not a project)
Best CTA: 2-minute scorecard
Likely objections: "I'm too small to be a target", "my tax software handles security", "no time before or during season", "I've never had a problem", "I can't afford another subscription or consultant", "I don't know what a WISP is or whether I need one"
Language: clients, returns, filing season, e-file, EFIN, PTIN, Pub 4557, written plan, safeguards, "protecting client data"
Avoid: EDR, SOC, zero trust, threat-actor jargon, enterprise-sounding terms, fear language, anything implying negligence, long checklists that feel like an audit

PERSONA: Bookkeeper (remote/hybrid)

Cares about: client trust (they hold bank, payroll, and financial access for many small businesses); working securely from home or on the road without clunky tools; keeping referrals and client relationships; looking credible next to CPAs and larger firms; not being the weak link in a client's finances
Triggers: a client asking "how do you protect our data?" or sending a security questionnaire, onboarding a client with bank or payroll access, a suspicious login alert or compromised email account, hiring a subcontractor or virtual assistant, moving to remote/hybrid or a new device, a CPA partner requiring vendor security standards 
Best angle: remote access, client portals, Microsoft 365 security (lead with "what clients and CPA partners will start asking you to prove")
Best CTA: 2-minute scorecard
Likely objections: "I already use a password manager", "my clients haven't asked", "QuickBooks/Xero handles it", "I'm just one person or a tiny team", "I don't want to slow down my workflow", "no budget for security tools"
Language: clients, access, logins, permissions, client portal, month-end, reconciliation, "keeping client data safe," professional standards
Avoid: EDR, SOC, zero trust, heavy IT terminology, fear language, anything aimed at a corporate IT team

PERSONA: Payroll provider or service

Cares about: accurate, on-time payroll; client trust with SSNs, bank details, and wage data; meeting security expectations of clients, partners, and banks; avoiding fraud such as fake direct-deposit change requests
Triggers: a client or bank asking about security controls, a fraudulent direct-deposit change attempt, business email compromise targeting a client, onboarding a larger client with vendor security requirements, cyber insurance application or renewal 
Best angle: protecting employee data and preventing payment fraud, tied to vendor due-diligence expectations
Best CTA: 2-minute scorecard
Likely objections: "our payroll software vendor handles it", "we're too small to be a target", "we can't disrupt payroll cycles"
Language: clients, employees, payroll runs, direct deposit, sensitive data, controls, verification
Avoid: EDR, SOC, zero trust, fear language, anything implying payroll accuracy is at risk

PERSONA: Credit counselor

Cares about: client confidentiality (clients are often in financial distress); meeting funder, HUD, or accreditor requirements; protecting vulnerable clients from identity theft; limited nonprofit budget
Triggers: a funder or accreditor audit, a grant application requiring a security policy, a lost laptop or misdirected email with client data, new staff or volunteers with access to client files 
Best angle: safeguarding sensitive client financial information and satisfying funder or accreditor expectations
Best CTA: 2-minute scorecard
Likely objections: "we're a nonprofit with no budget", "we don't have IT staff", "our clients' data isn't that valuable"
Language: clients, counseling, confidentiality, accreditation, funders, privacy, "protecting the people we serve"
Avoid: EDR, SOC, zero trust, fear language, corporate or vendor-sales tone

PERSONA: Collections agency

Cares about: regulatory compliance (FDCPA, state licensing, client-mandated audits); meeting security requirements creditor clients impose by contract; protecting debtor data as a legal and reputational exposure; keeping creditor and lender contracts
Triggers: a creditor client's security questionnaire or audit, contract renewal with new security clauses, a regulator inquiry or complaint, a data incident at a peer agency 
Best angle: "your creditor clients will ask for proof of a written security program"
Best CTA: 2-minute scorecard
Likely objections: "our clients' auditors already review us", "we have an IT provider", "we can't afford downtime"
Language: accounts, portfolios, creditor clients, compliance, audit, licensing, safeguards
Avoid: EDR, SOC, zero trust, fear language, anything suggesting they mishandle debtor data
